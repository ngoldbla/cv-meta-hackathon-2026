Source: Firefighter Close Calls ("The Secret List"), "What's behind increase in
9-1-1 response times?", reporting FDNY statements on a delayed EMS call in
New York City (article page fetched 2026-11-02):

Verbatim passage (page text after tag stripping):
"According to the FDNY, the call was entered into the dispatch system at 2:50 am
and was coded as 'unknown.' The FDNY said the call was held for 31 minutes due
to a big spike in calls and limited units available. The call was assigned to a unit
at 3:21 and EMS workers arrived at 3:28."

Context in the article: the caller waited for her father's medical emergency; he was
transported in stable condition and later died at the hospital after going into
cardiac arrest; the family awaited autopsy results. No causal link between the hold
and the death is established in the article. FDNY also stated it responds to an
average of 100 to 150 calls an hour.
