Source: IAED Journal, "Time Is On Emergency Dispatcher's Side", describing the
University of Sheffield evaluation (Janette Turner) of NHS England's Ambulance
Response Programme (ARP) pilots. Page fetched 2026-11-02:
https://www.iaedjournal.org/time-is-on-emergency-dispatchers-side

Verbatim passage (page text after tag stripping; the Red/Green categories were
replaced by the new code set in phase 2 of the study):
"Category 1 (life-threatening calls): people needing treatment for life-threatening
illnesses or injuries with response in an average of seven minutes (90th percentile
of 15 minutes). Category 2 (emergency calls): potentially serious conditions that
might require rapid assessment, urgent on-scene intervention and/or urgent
transport, with response in an average of 18 minutes (90th percentile of 40
minutes). Category 3 (urgent calls): non-life-threatening problems that often can
be managed at the scene, with 90 percent responded to within 120 minutes.
Category 4 (less urgent calls): problems requiring assessment either face-to-face
or by telephone, with 90 percent responded to within 180 minutes."
