What we already have

A frozen judge

A simulator that replays real NYC 911 calls. It uses 3 years of data and matches 7 of 10 real-world checks. No loop can change it. A ledger records every change.

A strong baseline

b1 is a set of simple surge rules. It adds units when 2 calls wait, and it sends low-risk calls to a nurse line. Every new policy must beat b1.

Four locked claims

A claim stands only if it shows at least 5% less lateness in all 12 tests. We wrote the claims before the loops started.

Sealed test data

Omicron, COVID wave 1, normal 2016 days, and a hard stress pool. No loop has seen them. Each gets one test.

Four test loops

FULL, BLUE_ONLY, NO_CF and READABLE. FULL, NO_CF and READABLE also have a red loop that invents hard scenarios against the policy.

Fast cloud tools

Training runs on one H100 GPU on Modal. The cloud results match the local results exactly. Container limits keep the GPU from waiting.

What we found today

Fixed · speed

One training call waited 31 minutes for 26 seconds of work

Modal allows 100 containers for the whole workspace. Other loops filled them. Now each job type has its own limit, and 3 containers stay free for training. The wait is now about 5 seconds.

Fixed · labels

Training labels covered only midnight to 8 a.m.

A daily limit of 40 labels filled early. The loops never saw the busy daytime hours. Labels now cover the whole day at the same cost.

Fixed · labels

Labels for "hold this call" were not reliable

A label looks 3 hours ahead. We compared it with the full day on 48 decisions. They gave the same answer in 18 of 40 cases, about the same as chance. We now remove all hold labels. Labels for which unit to send are unchanged.

The loops rejected most learned policies because they held calls too often. This explains why the learned loops score no better than b1 so far.

What the results could mean
  • Learned policy wins: ambulances reach critical patients sooner in a surge. This is true in the simulator only.
  • Readable rules do as well: a service can write the rules into its procedures. It needs no model and no GPU. Today's results point most to this branch.
  • No method beats b1 by 5%: the simple surge rules in b1 stay the best advice. The study still publishes this result.
  • A policy fails on sealed data: the sealed tests stop an unsafe policy before it reaches real patients.

The work stays in the simulator. The score counts minutes late. It does not count deaths or hospital results. The data is from NYC, 2017 to 2019.
